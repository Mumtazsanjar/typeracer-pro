What Is a Typing Speed Test?

A typing speed test presents you with a passage of text and asks you to type it as accurately and quickly as possible within a set time. When time is up, it calculates two scores: your Words Per Minute (WPM) and your accuracy percentage.

These two numbers together tell you far more than either does alone. A high WPM with poor accuracy isn't productive — errors cost time in real work. A low WPM with near-perfect accuracy signals that technique is sound and speed will come with practice.

📐

How WPM Is Calculated

WPM doesn't count individual words — it normalises for word length. The standard method treats every 5 characters (including spaces and punctuation) as one "word." This makes results comparable across different texts and typists.

The formula is: (Correct characters ÷ 5) ÷ minutes elapsed. So if you type 360 correct characters in 60 seconds, your WPM is (360 ÷ 5) ÷ 1 = 72 WPM. TypeRacer Pro calculates this continuously so you can watch your rate in real time.

📊

What Is a Good Typing Speed?

Typing speed varies significantly by age, profession, and how much someone uses a keyboard day to day. A useful reference:

  • Under 30 WPM — below average; focused practice will help quickly
  • 30–50 WPM — around the general adult average
  • 50–70 WPM — good; comfortable for most professional tasks
  • 70–100 WPM — fast; typical of experienced touch typists
  • 100+ WPM — very fast; the result of years of deliberate practice

There's no single "correct" target. Focus on whether your speed meets your own needs and whether it's improving over time.

⏳

Which Test Duration Should You Choose?

Test length affects your result more than most people expect:

  • 30 seconds — captures your burst speed. Results can be higher than your sustained rate but are useful for quick checks.
  • 60 seconds — the most widely used standard. Balances speed and consistency well.
  • 120 seconds — the most reliable measurement. Any fatigue or inconsistency in technique shows up here.

For tracking genuine improvement over time, the 60s or 120s test gives the most honest picture.

🚀

How to Improve Your Typing Speed

Speed improves fastest when you build the right habits first:

  • Learn touch typing — assign each finger to its designated keys using home-row positioning (left: ASDF, right: JKL;). Never looking at the keyboard is the single biggest long-term improvement lever.
  • Accuracy before speed — forcing yourself to go faster than your accuracy allows just reinforces bad habits. Aim for 95%+ accuracy and let speed follow naturally.
  • Short daily sessions — 10 to 15 minutes every day beats a single long session once a week.
  • Progress through difficulty levels — start on Easy, move to Medium when accuracy is stable, then take on Hard.
  • Track your weak spots — notice which characters or combinations you consistently mistype and drill those specifically.
🎯

Why Accuracy Matters More Than Speed

Every error you make mid-typing costs more time than you saved going fast: you have to stop, backspace, and retype. At 60 WPM with 88% accuracy, your effective throughput is lower than typing at 48 WPM with 99% accuracy.

Beyond raw efficiency, accuracy matters in context. A typo in code can introduce a hard-to-find bug. An error in a professional email affects how you're perceived. Building an accurate foundation first means your speed improvements are immediately useful — not just impressive on a test.
